# A domain handover checklist for an independent news publisher

A news website can be technically ready while its domain remains a source of uncertainty. The publication may know its preferred name but not who controls the registration, receives renewal notices or can approve a connection change. For an independent reporter, these questions are easy to postpone during a busy launch. A simple handover record prevents that uncertainty from becoming a recurring operational problem. This guide focuses on responsibilities and verification rather than complicated configuration instructions.

## Identify the owner of each task

Write down the person responsible for choosing the publication name, purchasing the domain and connecting it to the website. One person may handle all three tasks, but they are still different tasks. Registration establishes the domain account arrangement. Connection makes the address lead to the intended service. Editorial identity explains to readers which publication they are visiting. Confusing these responsibilities can leave everyone assuming that somebody else has completed the final step.

Consider a reporter who asks a designer to arrange the launch. The designer may purchase the domain and build the logo, while the website provider handles the publishing service. Before launch, the reporter should know which account receives renewal notices and how support requests are routed. A brief written record is more useful than a long conversation that nobody can refer to when the designer is unavailable.

## Confirm the exact spelling

Read the proposed domain aloud and type it without copying it. Check whether a listener can understand the spelling. Repeated letters, unexplained numbers and unusual abbreviations can create avoidable mistakes. If your publication name uses Hindi, choose a consistent Latin spelling for the domain and supporting profiles. Readers should not have to guess whether two different spellings represent the same organisation.

Look at how the name appears in the logo, website header and contact details. If the trading name and operator name differ, explain the relationship accurately where appropriate. Do not imply an association with another publication simply because the names resemble each other. This is an identity check, not a promise that a particular domain will receive more traffic or perform better in search results.

## Separate service costs

Custom domain support means a platform can accommodate your chosen address under its service arrangements. It does not automatically establish that registration, renewal or every related service is included. Ask for the scope in clear language. Find out what happens when an existing domain is used, what information is required and whether any separate charges apply. Keep the answer alongside the subscription details.

Avoid choosing a larger publishing allowance merely because the domain question is unclear. Resolve the domain arrangements directly, then select the article and media capacity that matches your editorial workload.

## Record access without circulating secrets

Your handover record should identify the relevant provider and responsible account holder. It should not become a shared list of passwords. Store sensitive access information through an appropriate private arrangement and understand the account recovery process. If assistance is needed, use supported access options and limit the arrangement to the work being performed. Public chats and broadly shared screenshots are poor places for account secrets.

Check the contact details used for renewal and recovery. A number or email address belonging to someone who has left the publication can create unnecessary friction later. Assign a person to review notices and keep the details current. The purpose is straightforward continuity: the reporter should know where to go for help without searching through months of unrelated messages during a publishing deadline.

## Prepare a reader-facing identity check

After the address is connected, open the homepage yourself. Confirm that it shows the correct publication name and that the contact route belongs to your organisation. Then open an article directly using its full link. A visitor arriving from a shared story should understand the publication's identity without first returning to the homepage. Check that leftover sample names have been removed from visible areas.

Repeat the check on a phone. A logo that is clear on a large monitor may become difficult to read at a smaller size. The browser icon and page title can also help a reader recognise the site, but they should support accurate identity rather than replace it. Make sure an attractive header does not distract from a missing contact page or an unfinished article.

## Test three ordinary journeys

Choose one homepage link, one category link and one article link. Send those links to a person who was not involved in the setup and ask them to open each one. They should not need an explanation of which address is temporary or which page is the final version. Record unexpected redirects, missing pages or confusing labels with the exact affected link.

If the publication previously used another address, make a separate list of important old links. Ask the provider how the transition will be handled before advertising the new address widely. Do not assume a change of domain automatically preserves every existing link. This planning is particularly useful when old stories have already been shared in community groups, newsletters or printed promotional material.

## Define renewal and support follow-up

Choose a date to review renewal responsibility before the renewal itself becomes urgent. Confirm who receives notices and who approves the payment. This is separate from the process for paying the news platform subscription, even if the same person handles both. A small publication can keep the arrangement simple as long as the responsibility is explicit and the records are accessible to the authorised person.

Also record how to report a problem. A useful message includes the public address, what the reader sees and when the issue was noticed. Avoid sending repeated vague requests such as saying that everything is broken. Clear information helps the support team identify the relevant service and reproduce the problem. Keep credentials out of the report unless a secure, appropriate process specifically requires them.

## Finish with an observable result

The handover is complete when the intended address opens the intended publication, the important reader journeys work and the responsible person has the necessary operational information. A screenshot of a homepage alone does not establish all of that. Verify the result using real links and record any remaining questions instead of treating assumptions as completed tasks.

A well-organised domain arrangement does not write better news for you. It gives your reporting a consistent home and makes routine administration less distracting. For a solo publisher, that matters because the same working day must accommodate interviews, verification, writing and publication. A short checklist completed once can prevent a small technical uncertainty from repeatedly interrupting the work readers actually visit the website to find.
