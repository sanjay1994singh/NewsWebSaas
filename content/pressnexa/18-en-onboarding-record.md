# Keep an onboarding decision record for your news website

A website handover often involves several small decisions rather than one final instruction. A publisher changes a category name, chooses a different contact address or supplies a revised introduction. If those decisions remain scattered across messages, the person preparing the website may not know which version to use. A concise decision record can reduce that ambiguity without turning a small launch into a complicated project management exercise.

## Record decisions rather than every conversation

The record should capture the current answer to a relevant question. It does not need to reproduce an entire discussion. For example, note the approved publication name and the date it was confirmed. If several alternatives were considered, the website team usually needs the chosen version more than the history of every suggestion.

Keep supporting discussion where it belongs, but make the final instruction easy to locate. An independent reporter may be the only decision maker, yet the record still helps when work is spread across several days. Memory is less reliable than a short written statement when the same detail has been discussed more than once.

## Give each item a clear status

Use a small set of understandable labels such as confirmed, awaiting information and replaced. The labels should describe reality rather than create an impression that everything is finished. A contact address awaiting confirmation should not be treated as ready simply because the layout needs some text.

For each unresolved item, identify what would resolve it. A note saying that the publication introduction needs review is more useful when it also identifies who will read it. Avoid assigning an arbitrary completion time that the responsible person has not considered. The record is a coordination aid, not a way to hide uncertainty behind a confident date.

## Separate publication identity from operating details

The name readers see and the organisation operating the publication may be related without being identical. Record both accurately where relevant, along with the wording approved for public use. If the relationship needs explanation, supply a clear sentence. Do not leave the website team to infer it from an invoice or an old social profile.

Keep private operational details out of the public copy. The fact that a phone number appears in an internal conversation does not establish that it should be displayed on the contact page. Mark which contact route is intended for readers and confirm that someone monitors it. This distinction is useful even when one person handles both editorial and administrative work.

## Refer to complete versions of content

When an introduction or sample article changes, identify the complete replacement file. A sequence of small corrections in separate messages can be hard to apply accurately. Sending a clear final version gives the reviewer a single text to check and reduces the chance that one correction is missed.

Use filenames that help distinguish the material without relying on unexplained abbreviations. The record can point to the chosen version and identify the earlier one as replaced. It need not contain the full article itself. The goal is to make it obvious which source should be used when the page is prepared or reviewed.

## Describe the intended use of samples

A sample article can be intended for layout testing, review or actual publication. State which purpose applies. A website team cannot always determine that from the content alone. An article that looks complete may still be waiting for editorial confirmation, while a simple example may be approved for public use.

Include the appropriate headline, summary, body and image context when supplying a realistic sample. That allows a review of the actual presentation rather than a page filled with unrelated placeholder text. If a sample is not for public release, make that instruction visible in the decision record and confirm the relevant process with the provider.

## Track questions about included features

Write down a specific need before asking whether a feature is available. A request to publish a daily digital edition is clearer than a general request for an advanced website. A need to include YouTube reports is different from a need for every possible video service. Specific questions are easier to answer and less likely to produce mismatched expectations.

Record the answer with a reference to the current plan information or the provider's clarification. Press Nexa's [public plan comparison](https://pressnexa.live-app.in/saas/) distinguishes article allowances and formats such as blogs, video and ePaper. Check that information when selecting a plan. Do not interpret a general product description as confirmation that every feature is included in the chosen subscription.

## Keep the next action visible

For each open question, identify the next useful action. It might be confirming a public email address, supplying an approved logo or checking a sample page. The action should be concrete enough that someone can complete it and report a result. Saying that the website needs improvement does not give the same direction as identifying a particular incorrect label.

Review the open items before sending a follow-up message. Several questions may be resolved together, while others may depend on missing information. A compact message containing the relevant decisions is often easier to act on than many separate fragments. This does not remove the need for discussion; it makes the outcome of that discussion easier to use.

## Distinguish review from public release

The Press Nexa public FAQ describes subscription, onboarding, review and publication as separate states. A completed purchase therefore should not be treated as proof that every onboarding decision has been settled. Ask how readiness is communicated and what remains necessary before public release.

When a review page is available, compare it with the decision record. Check the publication identity, approved contact information and selected sample content. Record specific differences instead of assuming that an attractive appearance means every detail is correct. Visual presentation and accurate content are related parts of the handover, but neither establishes the other automatically.

## Close the record with confirmed outcomes

At handover, mark which decisions were implemented and which questions remain open. Keep the final version somewhere the responsible publisher can retrieve. It can later help explain why a category was named a certain way or which contact details were approved. Avoid retaining unnecessary sensitive material merely because it appeared during the setup process.

A useful decision record is brief, current and unambiguous. It does not guarantee a particular delivery time, but it can reduce avoidable back-and-forth caused by unclear instructions. For a small news publication, that leaves more attention available for the reporting and editorial work that will give the finished website its purpose.
