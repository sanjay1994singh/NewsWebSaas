# A practical launch checklist for a small news website

A fast news website launch begins before anyone changes a template or connects a domain. The publisher needs a clear name, a manageable reporting scope, usable content and an agreed route to publication. When these decisions arrive late, even a prepared publishing system cannot resolve every delay. This guide explains how a small newsroom can prepare its launch without confusing speed with an unfinished website. The aim is to remove avoidable waiting while preserving the checks that readers need.

## Define what the first version must do

Start with a short description of your publication. Name the area or subject you cover, the language you will use and the readers you want to help. A district news service might initially focus on local administration, public services and community reporting. It does not need empty sections for every topic imaginable. A smaller set of useful categories gives the editor a clearer daily workload and helps readers understand what they can expect.

Make a separate list of essential launch requirements and later improvements. Publishing articles under your own identity may be essential. A video section is useful when you already produce video. A daily digital newspaper needs a dependable edition workflow. Features without an immediate editorial purpose can wait. This distinction also makes a product demonstration more useful because you can test a real task instead of simply counting buttons in the dashboard.

## Prepare the publication identity

Decide on the publication name before ordering several versions of a logo. Check that the spelling is consistent across the website, contact details and social profiles. Prepare a readable logo, a short description and an email address that someone actually monitors. If you already have a domain, identify who manages it and who can update its settings. Do not share account passwords in public messages or include them in a launch document circulated widely.

Custom domain support does not necessarily mean domain registration is included in the subscription price. Ask which party purchases and renews the domain, what information is required to connect it and how ownership remains documented. These are practical delivery questions. Resolving them early helps avoid a situation where the website is prepared but nobody can complete the domain connection. Keep a record of the final hostname and the person responsible for renewal.

## Match the plan to the content

Estimate your monthly article workload using actual reporting capacity. Four articles per day over thirty days gives an example total of one hundred and twenty. Leave room for unusually busy days, but do not create filler to use an allowance. Then list whether you require blogs, YouTube videos, Shorts or an ePaper. These are separate requirements and should be checked individually against the current plan comparison.

Press Nexa's public comparison on 7 September 2026 listed News Starter with 150 monthly news articles and a custom domain. News Plus listed 300 articles with blogs and a custom domain. News Pro listed 1,500 articles with blogs, a custom domain, YouTube videos and Shorts. News Professional listed 10,000 articles and also included ePaper with 30 monthly editions. Treat this as a dated snapshot and confirm current limits before buying.

## Bring real sample content

Prepare several original reports that resemble the work you will publish after launch. Include a clear headline, a concise summary, the full report, an appropriate category and an image you have permission to use. Add a useful caption when the picture needs context. A real sample exposes practical questions that placeholder text hides, such as whether a long Hindi headline remains readable or whether a location needs clarification.

Ask another person to read the sample without explanation from the reporter. Can they identify what happened, where it happened and why it matters? Can they distinguish confirmed information from an allegation? Are names and dates consistent? A quick editorial pass before uploading is often simpler than correcting the same mistake across several published pages. A launch deadline should not turn an unverified statement into an apparently established fact.

## Make the organisation reachable

Prepare an About page that explains who operates the publication and what it covers. Provide a contact route for corrections and ordinary enquiries. Check that the address or form works rather than assuming it does. Information about privacy and other applicable site terms should describe the actual operation of the website. Avoid copying another publisher's pages when the named company, services or data practices do not match your own.

These pages are useful to readers beyond the launch date. Someone may want to report a factual error, ask about an old article or understand who is responsible for a story. Assign a person to review incoming messages and decide how corrections are recorded. A contact page that nobody monitors creates a different kind of maintenance problem, even when the website itself loads correctly.

## Test the reading journey

Open the prepared site on a phone and a larger screen. Follow the route from the homepage to a category and then to a complete article. Check that the headline is visible, paragraphs are comfortable to read and navigation does not hide the story. Test images and links using the actual sample content. Record the problem and the affected page so the person fixing it can reproduce the issue.

For a video publication, check the surrounding text as well as playback. For an ePaper publication, check the edition date and whether the sample pages remain legible when enlarged. Test contact links and make sure an unpublished sample cannot be mistaken for a current report. These checks do not establish a universal performance score, but they can reveal obvious obstacles before readers encounter them.

## Agree the route to publication

The Press Nexa public FAQ distinguishes payment, onboarding, review and publication. A subscription purchase therefore should not be interpreted as a promise that a public website appears immediately. Ask what information is still required, who reviews it and how you will know the site is ready. Record any delivery estimate with its dependencies, especially when domain access or publisher content is still outstanding.

Finish with a simple handover: confirm the public address, the person publishing the first stories and the way to report a problem. Keep the first week manageable and review actual reader questions before expanding. For current commercial details, consult the [Press Nexa plans page](https://pressnexa.live-app.in/saas/). Good preparation makes faster delivery more achievable because the important decisions and usable materials are ready when they are needed.
